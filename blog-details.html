The Complete Bonsai Care Handbook for Central India

Practical guidelines on moisture control, pot aeration, light placement, and branch training from the masters at Nature Nursery, Indore.

Bonsai is not a stunted or starved dwarf plant—it is a healthy, vigorous tree kept compact through deliberate horticultural artistry, root trimming, and mindful pot confinement. In the climate of Central India, characterized by scorching dry pre-monsoon summers and concentrated rainy spells, caring for a bonsai requires nuanced understanding.

1. The Golden Rule of Watering: The Touch Test

The number one killer of bonsai trees is a rigid watering calendar (such as "I water every Tuesday and Friday"). Indoor heating, dry winds, and seasonal temperature swings make scheduled watering dangerous.

How to check: Insert your index finger about 1 cm into the potting soil. If it feels completely dry and gritty, it is time to water. If it feels damp and cool, wait another 24 hours.

When you do water, water thoroughly until you see excess liquid draining freely from the holes underneath the ceramic pot. This flushes out stale salts and pulls fresh oxygen into the root zone.

2. Sunlight and Micro-Climates

Trees are naturally outdoor organisms. While indoor species like Ficus Microcarpa and Carmona adapt well to modern living rooms, they still require intense, indirect natural light:

  • Ficus Microcarpa: Thrives near east-facing windows receiving morning sun. Rotate the pot 90 degrees every fortnight so all sides grow evenly.
  • Chinese Elm: Prefers an outdoor balcony or veranda with partial shade during harsh May afternoons.
  • Lagerstroemia: Needs 4-6 hours of direct sun to set blossom buds.

3. Soil Composition and Drainage

Never use ordinary heavy clay garden soil in a shallow bonsai pot. Standard black soil cakes up like concrete in the summer and retains stagnant mud in the monsoon. Our nursery recommends an equal blend of pumice or volcanic grit, river sand, cocopeat, and aged leaf compost.

4. Pruning and Maintenance Trimming

To maintain the miniature silhouette, regular maintenance trimming is necessary. When a shoot grows 4 to 6 new leaves, prune it back to 2 leaves using clean, sharp bonsai shears. Always trim just above an outward-facing leaf bud to direct new growth away from the trunk.
